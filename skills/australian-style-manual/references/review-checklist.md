# Review checklist and source map

Use this map during an assessment. Open the relevant official guidance before applying its rules; follow links within each topic for exceptions and specialist cases. These prompts support a complete review without reproducing the manual. Mark a dimension not applicable when the content does not contain it.

## Meaning and language

| Dimension | Review prompts | Official guidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| User need and tone | Can the audience find the answer and act on it? Remove unnecessary complexity, explain unfamiliar terms and use a tone appropriate to the situation. Preserve necessary technical precision. | [Clear language and writing style](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/writing-and-designing-content/clear-language-and-writing-style) |
| Sentences | Prefer direct sentences and active voice where they clarify who does what. Split overloaded sentences without losing conditions or relationships. Assess readability in context rather than treating a score as proof of compliance. | [Sentences](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/writing-and-designing-content/clear-language-and-writing-style/sentences) |
| Australian spelling | Use the organisation's chosen Australian dictionary consistently: the manual recommends Macquarie or the Australian concise Oxford dictionary. Check the first-listed spelling and any justified alternative. Do not assume British spelling always equals Australian spelling or apply automatic suffix substitutions. If dictionary access is unavailable, flag uncertain words. | [Spelling](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/spelling) |
| Inclusion | Check respectful references to First Nations people, disability, neurodiversity, age, culture, gender and sexuality. Consult the relevant subtopic; avoid assumptions about identity and blanket terminology replacements. | [Inclusive language](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/accessible-and-inclusive-content/inclusive-language) |
| Editorial completeness | Check consistency, missing information, contradictions and every supporting content element, not only body text. | [Editing and proofreading](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/writing-and-designing-content/editing-and-proofreading) |

## Structure and navigation

| Dimension | Review prompts | Official guidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Headings | Use descriptive sentence-case headings with important words early. Preserve proper nouns and acronyms. Avoid terminal full stops. Check logical heading levels, a single main heading on a webpage and the different conventions for print. | [Headings](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/structuring-content/headings) |
| Links | Make destination and purpose clear when link text is read alone. Replace vague link labels using the actual destination. Check download information against the real file; do not invent sizes or formats. Preserve link targets unless a verified correction is in scope. | [Links](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/structuring-content/links) |
| Lists | Choose numbered lists for sequences and bullets for unordered items. Check parallel grammar and the appropriate punctuation for sentence, fragment and stand-alone lists; do not force one punctuation pattern on all lists. | [Lists](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/structuring-content/lists) |
| Other structures | Check paragraph focus and ordering, table headings and relationships, and whether callouts help readers. Follow the relevant topic for paragraphs, tables and text boxes. | [Structuring content](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/structuring-content) |

## Editorial conventions

| Dimension | Review prompts | Official guidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Punctuation and capitals | Check minimal capitalisation, apostrophes, commas, hyphens, dashes and sentence endings in context. Preserve official names. Follow each mark's own guidance; do not indiscriminately strip punctuation. | [Punctuation](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation) |
| Quotation marks | Check single quotation marks for ordinary direct quotations and double marks for a quotation within a quotation, plus block-quotation and punctuation conventions. Preserve the quoted words. | [Quotation marks](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/quotation-marks) |
| Shortened forms | Check whether unfamiliar abbreviations need expansion, whether they are necessary, and consistency across independently read pages. Consult exceptions for familiar forms, symbols and units. | [Shortened words and phrases](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/shortened-words-and-phrases) |
| Numbers | Choose numerals or words for the context; do not impose a blanket ‘spell out one to nine’ rule. Check grouping, ordinals and sentence beginnings using the detailed guidance. | [Choosing numerals or words](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/choosing-numerals-or-words) |
| Dates and time | Use day–month–year order in ordinary Australian body text, such as ‘16 September 2026’, without an ordinal suffix or comma. Check time notation, ranges and financial-year exceptions. Preserve machine date formats and actual instants. Flag ambiguous numeric dates rather than guessing their order. | [Dates and time](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/dates-and-time) |
| Quantities and contact details | Check currencies, percentages, decimals, fractions, measurement symbols, spacing and telephone number grouping against the relevant subtopic. Preserve values, currency identity, precision and working telephone links. | [Numbers and measurements](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements) |
| Names, titles and references | Check official names, titles, honours, italics and attribution using the applicable topic. Preserve source identity and distinguish reference formatting from ordinary prose conventions. | [Grammar, punctuation and conventions](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions), [Referencing and attribution](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/referencing-and-attribution) |

## Content types and accessibility

| Dimension | Review prompts | Official guidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Forms and interface text | Check labels, instructions, required information, input format guidance and actionable errors. Review validation, success and empty states as well as the default screen. Keep instructions accurate to actual behaviour and visible labels consistent with accessible names. | [Forms](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/content-types/forms) |
| Images, audio and video | Inspect the asset and its purpose before assessing alternatives. Check meaningful alt text, decorative-image treatment, captions and transcripts through the relevant topic. Report missing or inaccessible assets rather than fabricating descriptions. | [Content types](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/content-types) |
| Documents and other formats | Follow the relevant guidance for reports, PDFs, email, letters, social media and other formats. Plain-language editing alone does not produce a validated Easy Read resource. | [Content types](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/content-types) |
| Accessibility and inclusion | Check semantic structure, understandable instructions and equivalent information. Separate content fixes from interaction, visual-design or technical accessibility issues that need additional assessment. | [Accessible and inclusive content](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/accessible-and-inclusive-content) |

## Context checks

Use these cases to check editorial judgement, not as automatic replacement rules:

- Change ‘September 16th, 2026’ to ‘16 September 2026’ in ordinary body text. Keep an ISO timestamp in a data field intact.
- Change a generic heading ‘Application Requirements’ to ‘Application requirements’. For a named publication, preserve its words and identity but consult the specific title and referencing rules before changing capitalisation or formatting; website names and titles of works can follow different conventions.
- Improve ‘Click here’ using the verified destination, for example ‘Read the application guide’. Do not change the destination just to match invented link text.
- Review the displayed word ‘Color’ for Australian spelling, but preserve a CSS property named `color` and a placeholder such as `{colorName}`.
- Retain ‘You must apply by …’ when it expresses an obligation. A friendlier tone does not justify changing it to ‘You can apply by …’.
- Leave an already suitable sentence unchanged. An audit measures coverage and necessary corrections, not the volume of rewritten text.
